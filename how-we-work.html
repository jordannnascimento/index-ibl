How We Work

The method behind the sourcing program.

Index-IBL works through a sourcing-office model designed to bring structure, selection discipline, and execution control to sourcing programs.

This page explains how that model operates.

Regional operating model

Index-IBL works under a Latin America sourcing umbrella, with its strongest execution depth across South and Central America, including Mexico.

Operationally, Brazil remains the core of the structure. It anchors sourcing operations, supplier development, quality supervision, engineering coordination, procurement management, and broader execution support.

Alongside that operational core, the company maintains strategic commercial presence in the United States to support relationships and business development.

Supplier qualification comes before supplier presentation

Manufacturers are not introduced simply because they make a relevant item.

Before a supplier is presented, the question is whether that supplier can realistically support the category, the product, the program, and the relationship over time.

That discipline is one of the defining features of the model.

What qualification includes

Supplier qualification is tied to the real requirements of the sourcing program.

Review may include
  • certification
  • documentation
  • regulatory fit
  • technical suitability
  • quality standards
  • operational capability
  • commercial fit
  • ethical suitability
  • financial suitability

The point is not to review these factors in isolation. It is to assess whether the supplier can support the program in a commercially and operationally sustainable way.

Governance across the sourcing process

Once a sourcing program is active, discipline still has to hold.

  • buyer requirements
  • supplier capability
  • product and specification expectations
  • production realities
  • quality needs
  • execution continuity

This is where sourcing becomes more than an introduction and starts functioning as a program.

Execution logic

Reliable sourcing depends on continuity.

That is why the model includes ongoing coordination around the parts of the process that often weaken if no one is managing them closely enough: communication flow, requirement alignment, production follow-up, quality coordination, and supply-chain continuity.

The aim is practical: reduce avoidable friction and support a steadier execution path.

Same method, different category contexts

The method remains the same across the company’s selected opportunity universes:

  • Building, Improvement & Material Supply
  • Industrial & Manufacturing Supply
  • Food, Ingredients & Agro-Industrial Supply

What changes is the category context. What remains constant is the discipline of qualification, governance, and coordinated follow-through.

Why this matters

Many sourcing problems begin after the opportunity is identified — when supplier fit is weaker than expected, communication breaks down, or follow-up is inconsistent.

Index-IBL’s model is built to reduce those gaps and support sourcing programs that can hold over time.
